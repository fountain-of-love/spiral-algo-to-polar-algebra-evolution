## 1. Introduction

The Resonant Capacity Law (RCL) reframes Einstein’s *E = m c²* as a transdisciplinary law of transformation capacity. By interpreting the squared constant as a **resonance multiplier**, the RCL extends the logic of energy equivalence into the realms of biology, economics, and information systems. The result is a unified framework in which the capacity for transformation—whether physical, living, social, or technological—emerges from the interplay between **substrate, flow, and resonance.**

This final phase synthesizes the theoretical, comparative, and philosophical implications of the RCL, positioning it as both a scientific and systemic principle for understanding complex adaptive systems.

## 2. Interpretation of Resonance

### 2.1 Resonance as Feedback-Amplified Coherence

In every domain examined, the squared term represents more than magnitude—it represents **feedback**. Resonance occurs when a flow interacts with itself, producing self-reinforcing coherence. Physically, this manifests as wave harmonics; biologically, as metabolic or ecological cycles; socially, as trust loops and cultural coherence; computationally, as recursive optimization.

Thus, *resonance* becomes the key operator linking energy, organization, and adaptation:

> Resonance = feedback × coherence × amplification.

### 2.2 Structural Universality

* In **physics**, *c²* denotes the resonance of spacetime itself—the speed of causality squared.
* In **biology**, metabolic feedback loops create exponential vitality.
* In **economics**, networked trust and communication multiply productivity.
* In **AI**, recursive learning generates accelerated intelligence scaling.

Across these contexts, resonance acts as a universal amplifier of transformation capacity.
