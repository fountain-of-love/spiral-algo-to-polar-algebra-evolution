## 1️⃣ What “Geometry” Means in Computational Optimization Papers

When papers on **Euclidean TSP** or **geometric PTAS** speak of *geometry*, they typically mean the **Cartesian** kind:

| Aspect | Standard Interpretation |
|:--|:--|
| **Domain** | A finite set of points in \( \mathbb{R}^d \) (usually 2D or 3D). |
| **Metric** | Euclidean distance: \( d(p,q) = \sqrt{(x_p - x_q)^2 + (y_p - y_q)^2} \). |
| **Structure used** | Lines, segments, and angles — essentially linear geometry. |
| **Algorithmic tools** | Partitioning (quadtree), grids, triangulations, bounded-angle MSTs, planar separators. |
| **Continuity notion** | Piecewise-linear continuity: paths of straight edges with limited crossing or angle. |

Thus, *academic geometry* usually means **linear and angular relations** among Cartesian coordinates.

Rotations, radii, and circular symmetries are **derived quantities**, not **primitive operators**.

> That’s why GAO’s explicit use of rotational coherence and radius–angle coupling lies *outside* the classical Euclidean-PTAS toolbox.  
> It’s a **polar geometry**, not a Cartesian one.
